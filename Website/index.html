01 — what happens

1

It arrives where you already are

Not a tab you switch to. A panel over Figma, over Excel, over the VPN client with the settings nobody can find. It captures that screen — and leaves itself out of the picture, or the model reads Cairn instead of your work.

2

You talk, and watch it hear you

Audio streams while you are still mid-sentence, so words land as you say them rather than arriving in a lump afterwards. You see a mishearing before it becomes the question, which matters more than it sounds.

3

The answer is drawn on the desk

Your screen dims, a cursor lands on the control, and each step is spoken. No screenshot with a red circle drawn on it, no "click the button near the top right" — the actual pixel, on the actual screen, on the monitor you asked from.

The credential and the screenshot are both fetched before they are needed, so neither is charged to the wait you actually notice. The 8 seconds a new answer takes is the model thinking, and nothing else.

05 — what it does not do

It is not watching you

A screenshot is taken at the moment you ask, used for that one answer, and not stored. Nothing is captured in between.

It holds no keys

The API keys live on the server. Unpacking the executable gets you a copy of the interface and nothing else.

It stays out of its own shot

The overlay is excluded from screen capture at the OS level. Without that the model reads Cairn's own window and starts explaining Cairn to you.

It leaves nothing behind

One file. No installer, no registry entries unless you switch on Start with Windows. Delete it and it is gone.

06 — the first minute

Windows says it can't verify the publisher

It can't — the binary is unsigned, because a certificate costs a few hundred dollars a year. Choose More info → Run anyway, or build it yourself from the source.

I pressed Ctrl+Space and nothing happened

Ctrl+Space is also the IME toggle for Japanese, Chinese and Korean input. If something already owns it, Cairn falls to Ctrl+Shift+Space, then Alt+Space, then Ctrl+Alt+C — and tells you which one it took, in the panel and in the tray tooltip.

It says it can't reach the server

Open the tray icon and choose Check connection. It will name the actual cause — a blocked domain, a proxy, antivirus inspecting HTTPS, or a system clock wrong enough to fail certificate checks.

Does it work in any app, or just a browser?

Any app. It is a desktop program, not an extension — which is the whole reason it isn't a web app. A browser can only see a tab you explicitly share with it.

I have two monitors

It dims only the screen you asked on. Documentation open on the other one stays readable.
